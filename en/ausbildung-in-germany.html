PREPARATION BEFORE APPLYING

What to clarify before you apply

Vocational training is not one single route. Before applying, it is useful to understand the training form, the occupational field, your German language level, your education and documents, whether you already have a training place, and how living costs may be covered. These are preparation topics, not a decision about whether you will be admitted or receive a residence title.

Choose a field to explore

Start with fields that fit your interests, previous education and practical preferences. Entry requirements and available places can differ by occupation, region, provider and employer.

Build your German-language preparation

German is important for learning, practical work and everyday communication. The required level can depend on the route and the particular programme, school or employer.

Organise your education and documents

Certificates, language evidence, translations and other documents may be relevant. The exact list depends on the programme, training form and later procedure.

Identify your current starting point

A concrete training place, your funding situation and your current residence situation can affect which questions need clarification next.

HOW VOCATIONAL TRAINING WORKS

What vocational training in Germany can look like

Vocational training in Germany is often called Ausbildung. It is a route into a recognised occupation that combines learning with practical preparation for working life. The organisation of training depends on the programme: some programmes are company-based and dual, while others are school-based with practical placements.

Company-based / dual training

Dual training normally alternates practical training in a company with teaching at a vocational school. A company-based training relationship normally includes a monthly training allowance, but the amount depends on the occupation, training year and contractual conditions.

School-based training

School-based training takes place mainly at a vocational school and can include practical placements or internships. Many school-based programmes do not pay a training allowance, although profession-specific paid exceptions exist.

Programme-dependent duration

Duration depends on the training form and the individual programme. Check the programme description or training regulation instead of assuming one duration applies to every occupation.

Preparation before a decision

The programme, provider, language level, education, documents, training place and funding situation all matter. They need to be clarified in the relevant process and do not create an automatic result.

TRAINING FORMS

Compare dual and school-based training before you choose

The two forms can lead to different practical questions. Compare the learning setting, the evidence of a training place, the likely payment pattern, education requirements and how living costs must be demonstrated.

Dual training: company practice and vocational school

In a dual programme, practical training in a company is combined with vocational-school teaching. A training contract is commonly relevant as evidence of a concrete place. The training allowance is normally paid by the company, but its amount is not the same as a decision about whether livelihood is secure for residence purposes.

School-based training: learning at a vocational school

In a school-based programme, learning takes place mainly at a vocational school and can include practical placements. Completed general school education and official recognition of the school-leaving qualification are relevant; the exact requirement depends on the profession. Many programmes do not pay an allowance, so funding must be considered separately.

Do not assume that a rule about a company-based programme automatically applies to a school-based programme. Confirm the requirements with the particular school, provider, employer and competent authority.

LANGUAGE, DOCUMENTS AND FUNDING

Clarify the conditions that apply to your training route

Language, qualifications, documents, a concrete training place and funding are related questions, but they are not the same question. The applicable conditions depend on the programme and residence route.

German language

For the §16a route with a concrete training place, current Federal Government guidance says that German at B1 is normally required if the training institution has not already assessed the required language skills and no preparatory German course is planned. A school, provider or employer may set different or higher requirements for a particular programme. B1 is not a universal guarantee of admission or a universal rule for every training situation.

Education and documents

For dual training, German law does not prescribe one specific school-leaving qualification; the training company sets its own application criteria. Recognition of a foreign school certificate can become relevant if a company requires a particular qualification. For school-based training, completed general school education and official recognition of the school-leaving qualification are required, with the exact level depending on the profession. School-certificate recognition is not the same as recognition of a foreign professional qualification.

A place already found, or a place still to be found

The §16a vocational-training route concerns an identified company-based/dual or school-based training programme. Official visa guidance requires evidence of a specific training place. If no place has yet been found, §17 is the separate route for seeking a place for qualified vocational training; its conditions are different and must not be assumed to apply to every applicant.

Training allowance and livelihood are separate

A training allowance is not the same as proof that living costs are secure. For eligible dual BBiG/HwO training relationships that begin in 2026, statutory monthly gross minimum-remuneration floors are €724 in year one, €854 in year two, €977 in year three and €1,014 in year four. These figures are legal floors in a defined scope. They are not average pay, net income, a general school-based entitlement or proof that livelihood is secure.

For a company-based/dual §16a training route, current 2026 guidance treats livelihood as secure when the allowance is at least €1,048 gross or €822 net per month. If the allowance is lower, recognised additional evidence may be relevant. For school-based training, the current 2026 benchmark is at least €959 net per month available; where accommodation is provided free of charge, the required amount is reduced by €380 per month. These are route-specific financial-security benchmarks, not salaries or individual visa decisions.

A PRACTICAL PREPARATION SEQUENCE

A practical sequence before you apply

The order can vary by programme and circumstance. Use these steps to organise preparation, not as a promise that every application follows the same path.

1. Explore the occupation and training form

Compare the work environment, the learning setting and the entry requirements of the programmes that interest you.

2. Build language and document readiness

Identify the language evidence, school documents, translations and other materials the particular programme or training provider asks for.

3. Clarify whether you already have a training place

A company-based contract or a school-based place changes the route questions. If no place is available yet, do not assume that the requirements for training with a place also apply to searching for one.

4. Plan funding separately from remuneration

Check how living costs must be evidenced for the relevant route. A training allowance, a statutory minimum allowance and a livelihood benchmark serve different purposes.

5. Use the relevant application and residence process

Apply to the programme, school or training company that interests you. For residence questions, use current official guidance and the competent authority’s instructions for your situation.

6. Recheck time-sensitive conditions

Monetary thresholds, programme requirements and administrative guidance can change. Confirm the current conditions before relying on them for an application or residence process.

OCCUPATIONAL FIELDS

Explore fields before you choose a programme

Vocational training covers many fields. The examples below are starting points for exploration, not a ranking of opportunities and not a statement that a place is available in a particular field.

Health and care

Roles can involve supporting patients, residents or care teams in hospitals, care settings and other health services. Training form, admission requirements and remuneration arrangements vary by programme.

Electrical, technical and mechatronics fields

Technical programmes can involve installing, maintaining or testing systems and equipment. Check the specific occupation, training company or school, language needs and practical work setting.

Skilled trades, construction and logistics

These fields can include practical work with materials, buildings, transport or supply processes. Requirements and available training places differ by occupation and region.

Hospitality and food services

Programmes can involve guest service, organisation, food preparation or hotel operations. The required language level and work environment should be checked for the individual programme.

IT and digital systems

Digital and technical programmes can include hardware, networks, systems or user support. Entry requirements and practical tasks depend on the specific occupation and provider.

Other recognised occupations

Your preferred field may be outside these examples. Use the programme name and the responsible provider to research the exact requirements, duration and learning setting.

RESIDENCE ROUTES AND LATER STEPS

Understand the route before travel and the options after successful training

Residence rules depend on whether you already have a training place, the training form, your nationality and current status, and the conditions of the relevant residence title. This page gives orientation only and cannot determine which title applies to an individual case.

If you have a specific training place

The §16a vocational-training route is for undertaking an identified company-based/dual or school-based programme. Official visa guidance requires evidence of a specific training place. Language, livelihood and other applicable conditions still need to be met in the relevant process.

If you do not yet have a training place

§17 is a separate route for seeking a place for qualified vocational training. It may be available to a person who is under 35 and who meets the prescribed school-certificate, German-language and secured-livelihood conditions. Current official guidance uses German at B1 and own funds of at least €1,091 net per month in 2026 for this route. The permit may be granted for up to nine months, permits employment up to 20 hours per week and permits trial employment up to a total of two weeks. These conditions do not guarantee a training place or a residence-title decision.

The Opportunity Card is a different title

The Opportunity Card is a separate job- and recognition-search residence title. It is not a vocational-training permit. A holder may apply for a training place and may use qualifying training-directed trial employment within the Card’s own legal limits. If a suitable training place is found, the holder must apply for the proper vocational-training residence permit, normally under §16a, before the Card expires and meet that route’s separate conditions.

After successful qualified training

After successful completion of qualified vocational training in Germany during a §16a stay, §20 can provide a residence permit to seek employment for up to 18 months if secured subsistence and the other applicable conditions are met. This is not an automatic job, continued residence or settlement outcome. A separate rule applies to certain health and care assistant or helper training, so the ordinary 18-month period is not universal.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Common questions about vocational training in Germany

  • Can I start vocational training in Germany as an international applicant?▼

    International applicants can explore vocational-training programmes in Germany, but the applicable requirements depend on the training form, programme, provider, nationality, current residence status and the relevant route. Confirm the applicable entry and residence process with current official guidance and the competent authority. A page cannot determine an individual case.

  • Is German at B1 always enough?▼

    No. For the §16a route with a concrete training place, B1 is normally required where the training institution has not tested the required skills and no preparatory German course is planned. A school, employer or programme can require a different or higher level. For the separate §17 training-place-search route, current official guidance uses B1.

  • Do I always need recognition of my school certificate?▼

    No. For dual training, German law does not prescribe one specific school-leaving qualification; the company sets its criteria, and recognition can be relevant if it requires a particular qualification. For school-based training, completed general school education and official recognition of the school-leaving qualification are required. The exact requirement depends on the profession.

  • Do people receive pay during vocational training?▼

    Company-based/dual training normally includes a monthly training allowance. Most school-based programmes do not pay an allowance, although profession-specific paid exceptions exist. A training allowance is separate from the question of whether livelihood is secure for a residence route.

  • What if I do not yet have a training place?▼

    Do not assume that a route for an identified training programme also covers a search situation. §17 is the separate route for seeking a place for qualified vocational training when no place has yet been found. Its under-35, school-certificate, German-language, livelihood, duration and work conditions apply specifically to that route.

  • Is there an age limit for vocational training?▼

    There is no general age limit for starting vocational training. The under-35 condition belongs specifically to the §17 route for seeking a training place and is not a general age limit for training itself.

  • Can I use the Opportunity Card for vocational training?▼

    The Opportunity Card is not a vocational-training permit. It can be relevant because a holder may apply for a training place and may use qualifying training-directed trial employment within the Card’s limits. Actual training requires the appropriate vocational-training permit, normally §16a, and the separate conditions for that route.

  • What can happen after successful training?▼

    After successful completion of qualified vocational training in Germany during a §16a stay, a §20 residence permit to seek employment can be possible for up to 18 months if the applicable conditions, including secured subsistence, are met. It does not guarantee employment, a further residence title or settlement.
