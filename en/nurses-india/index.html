Government programme

Triple Win Programme — Kerala & Telangana

A government-to-government recruitment channel run by Germany's Federal Employment Agency (ZAV) and GIZ, in partnership with each state's own agency.

Qualified Nurses

Triple Win — Registered Nurses

For nurses who already hold a recognised nursing qualification (GNM, B.Sc Nursing, or equivalent) and are ready to work. Kerala candidates apply through NORKA Roots; Telangana candidates apply through TOMCOM.

Nursing Trainee / Ausbildung

Triple Win — Trainee Programme

A separate track for candidates without a nursing qualification yet, who complete a 3-year vocational nursing training (Ausbildung) in Germany. Eligibility and entry requirements differ from the qualified-nurse track.

How to Apply

Applications are submitted directly through the official state agency for your region:

  • Kerala: NORKA Roots — norkaroots.org
  • Telangana: TOMCOM — tomcom.telangana.gov.in

Eligibility criteria — age, professional experience, and required German level — vary between application rounds and are set by NORKA Roots, TOMCOM, and the Federal Employment Agency. Always check the current call for applications on the official website before preparing your documents.

What "No Placement Fees" Means

The Triple Win programme itself charges no placement or recruitment fees. However, personal costs may still apply depending on the intake — for example, document translation, notarisation, and travel to interviews or exams. Check the current call for applications for the conditions of your round.

General Process

  1. Official application through NORKA Roots or TOMCOM
  2. Eligibility screening and interview
  3. German language preparation (up to B1, sometimes B2 depending on the round)
  4. Employer matching with a German hospital or care facility
  5. Recognition and visa coordination
  6. Arrival and integration support in Germany
Berlin Gateway does not administer the Triple Win programme and cannot guarantee acceptance. We help explain how the pathway works and support candidates who are not eligible for it in exploring the individual recognition route below.

All other Indian states

Individual Recognition Route

For nurses from states outside the Triple Win partnership, or those who prefer to start the process independently.

Nursing Is a Regulated Profession

In Germany, you cannot practise or use the professional title Pflegefachperson without formal recognition of your qualification and the required professional licence (Berufserlaubnis). This applies regardless of your experience or the state you apply from.

Who Decides — and Where to Apply

Your application is submitted to the competent recognition authority in the German federal state where you intend to work — not to ZAB. You can identify the correct authority using the official Anerkennungs-Finder. Once granted, recognition is valid throughout Germany.

No Employer Required to Begin

You can start the recognition process from India, before securing a job offer. An employer becomes relevant later — for the actual job placement and, eventually, the work visa — but is not a requirement to submit your recognition application.

How Indian Qualifications Are Assessed

B.Sc Nursing, GNM, and ANM are evaluated individually — there is no automatic equivalence. The recognition authority reviews the duration and content of your training, theoretical and practical hours, and your professional experience against the German reference qualification (Pflegefachfrau/Pflegefachmann). ANM in particular is not treated as automatically equivalent to Pflegefachperson.

Documents Typically Requested

  • Diploma/degree certificate and academic transcript
  • Description of subjects, and theoretical/practical hours completed
  • Indian nursing council registration
  • Proof of professional experience, where applicable
  • Identity documents and CV
  • Certified translations, as required by the competent authority

Exact requirements vary by state and by case — the list above is indicative, not exhaustive.

Possible Outcomes

  • Full recognition
  • Recognition with identified differences (Defizitbescheid)
  • Anpassungslehrgang (supervised adaptation period)
  • Kenntnisprüfung (knowledge/skills examination)
  • In some cases, refusal or a request for additional documentation

Language and Other Requirements

Full professional practice generally requires German at B2 level, alongside proof of health fitness and professional reliability (Zuverlässigkeit). These are assessed separately from your qualification equivalence.

After you arrive

Your First Year in Germany

What happens between arrival and full recognition matters as much as the application itself.

Working as a Nursing Assistant First

Under Triple Win, new arrivals typically work as nursing assistants while their recognition is completed. Until you are recognised, you cannot work as a fully qualified Pflegefachperson. Which work you may do depends on your residence permit and your recognition status.

Residence Status Under Triple Win

  • For recognition through a placement agreed between the Federal Employment Agency and the partner country, the Residence Act provides a residence permit for recognition (section 16d, paragraph 4). It is issued for one year at first and can be extended by one year at a time, up to three years in total.
  • After recognition, the immigration authority decides which permit applies next. This depends on your professional licence (Berufsausübungserlaubnis) and your concrete job; it does not change automatically.
  • A settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) is possible later. The general rule is five years with further conditions (section 9). Skilled workers can qualify after three years with a permit under sections 18a, 18b, 18d or 18g, subject to 36 months of pension contributions, sufficient German and other conditions (section 18c). Which rule applies to you depends on your permit history.
  • Family reunification depends on your residence permit and on the family member. Ask the immigration authority before you plan.

Sources: Federal Employment Agency, Triple Win India page; Residence Act (AufenthG) sections 9, 16d and 18c. Individual cases are decided by the immigration authority.

Visa options

Which Visa Applies to Nurses?

Nurses from India need a residence permit to work in Germany. Which one fits depends on your recognition status and your job.

  • Skilled-worker residence permit (after recognition): the usual route once your qualification is recognised. It requires your professional licence (Berufsausübungserlaubnis) or its confirmation, and a concrete job offer.
  • Residence permit for a qualification measure (section 16d, paragraph 1): possible if the recognition authority finds that an adaptation or compensation measure is needed. It requires a suitable measure, German skills matching it and, as with most residence permits, that you can support yourself. Mainly work-based measures may need the consent of the Federal Employment Agency. It is issued for up to 24 months at first and can be extended by up to 12 months. Employment unrelated to the measure is limited to 20 hours per week; employment that is professionally connected to the later occupation may be allowed without this limit under the conditions of section 16d, paragraph 2.
  • Recognition partnership (section 16d, paragraph 3): a residence permit to complete recognition while working in a qualified job. It requires a concrete job offer, a written agreement with a suitable employer, a state-recognised foreign qualification of at least two years of training or a university degree, and German skills matching the job; the official portal names level A2 as the minimum. In nursing, the job may be an assistant role until your professional licence is granted, if the legal conditions are met (for example a collective-bargaining employer and pay and grading matching the target profession). It is issued for up to one year at first and can be extended to three years in total.
  • Work as a nursing assistant: an option if your nursing qualification is not recognised. This is a separate route. It requires either a state-recognised German nursing-assistant training or a foreign qualification that the competent state authority has found equivalent to it (section 22a of the Employment Ordinance). A job offer alone is not enough.
  • EU Blue Card: the usual route for the pathway described here is a skilled-worker residence permit. The EU Blue Card may be available for qualifying academic nursing roles if your qualification and the job meet the requirements. Make it in Germany lists academic and comparable nursing and midwifery professionals as a shortage occupation. For shortage occupations the lower salary threshold of €45,934.20 gross per year (2026) applies, and it requires the approval of the Federal Employment Agency.

Sources: Residence Act (AufenthG) section 16d and Employment Ordinance (BeschV) section 22a; Make it in Germany, visa for recognition, recognition partnership and EU Blue Card. Which permit fits your case is decided by the German mission and the immigration authority.

Demand and pay

Why Germany Needs Nurses — and How Pay Works

In 2025, an average of around 31,000 vacancies in nursing occupations were registered with Germany's Federal Employment Agency.

How Pay Works

Pay is set by your employment contract or the collective agreement that applies to your employer. It depends on your role, the federal state, and whether you are already recognised or still working as a nursing assistant. Ask for the draft contract before you decide.

For current figures, use the Federal Employment Agency's Entgeltatlas, which shows monthly gross medians of full-time employees subject to social insurance. We do not quote salary figures on this page, because published ranges differ by source, state and employer type.
